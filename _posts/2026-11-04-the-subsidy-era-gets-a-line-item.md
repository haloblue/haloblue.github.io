title: "the subsidy era gets a line item"
---

Every platform shift runs on somebody else's money for a while. Uber rides in 2015 cost less than the gas. Cloud egress was free until it wasn't. And for the last three years, AI tokens have been priced for adoption, not for margin.

I've written big checks into a private business, so I'll say the quiet part: dudes like me want the money back, and then some. Not eventually. On a schedule. That's not villainy — it's the deal. Capital is patient right up until there's a filing deadline, and then patience becomes a disclosure item.

Which is why Anthropic's S-1 is the most interesting security-adjacent document of the quarter, even though it never uses the word "subsidy." Read the amended filing's third-quarter numbers and watch the tension: price cuts and discounts took roughly $600 million off Q3 revenue. Compute costs rose 77% while revenue rose 47%. The quarter swung to a $567 million operating loss after a profitable Q2, and the filing stacks up hundreds of billions in compute commitments behind it. The company says its newest model cut customer cost-per-task ~40% "while preserving our margins" — but the filing doesn't disclose revenue or cost per token, so nobody outside the building can verify that sentence.

Here's the thing about going public: the S-1 is the moment the subsidy stops being invisible. Private money can eat below-cost pricing quietly for years. Public money can't — every discount becomes a footnote, every margin claim gets an analyst assigned to it. The headline 85% gross margin looks great until you notice most revenue flows through the hyperscalers, and outside estimates put the real economic margin at half that. The market will converge on the truth, because now it's legally entitled to try.

So tokens get repriced. Maybe not as sticker increases — the industry has trained everyone to expect per-token prices to fall forever. It'll arrive as the discounts quietly not renewing. As the enterprise agreement that commits you to volume. As the "legacy model deprecation" that migrates you onto something with better unit economics for them. The price per token drops; the price per *outcome* finds its level.

If you've built your product, your SOC, or your budget on the assumption that inference is approximately free, you've built on a subsidy — and the people who funded it have now filed paperwork saying they'd like it back, with interest. I've met with several industry luminaries — people who are far smarter and more successful than I will ever be — and they are sitting directly in these very crosshairs right now.

None of this by the way is a knock on the model builders. It's just the part of the cycle we're in. The infrastructure people among us have seen this movie: adoption pricing, land grab, filing, repricing, consolidation. The only question is whether you planned for act four while enjoying act one.

Check your unit economics before someone else's S-1 does it for you.
